## O COCHO

**O começo de verdade ele chamou de pé de cocho.**
Milho torrado e moído, explicou, fermentando no caldo da cana.

![Fermento dourado espumando dentro de um tambor azul.](/images/editorial/cotegipe/fermento-tambor.webp)

![O mesmo fermento numa panela de inox.](/images/editorial/cotegipe/fermento-panela.webp)

O milho ficava ali até virar outra coisa.
No fim, era tão doce que havia marimbondos e abelhas em volta.

Fui atrás do nome depois, em artigo e em tese.

A literatura descreve o mesmo gesto com outro nome, fermento caipira: caldo de cana e milho moído, alimentados todo dia por cinco a sete dias, com a levedura que já vive no caldo, no equipamento e nas dornas.

Um artigo da UFSCar atribui o sabor da bebida a essas leveduras selvagens, e até a bactérias.
O mesmo artigo anota o custo: com tanto contaminante, a qualidade é difícil de controlar.

**Pé de cocho eu não achei escrito em lugar nenhum.**

# TROUGH, COPPER & OAK.

**Field notes from a mill in Cotegipe, Bahia: from the cane field to the barrel, by way of the starter the owner calls pé de cocho.**

> Science describes the method in papers and theses. The name the owner gives it I could not find written anywhere.

## THE TROUGH

**The real beginning, he called pé de cocho.**
Corn, toasted and ground, he explained, left to ferment in cane juice.

![Golden starter foaming inside a blue drum.](/images/editorial/cotegipe/fermento-tambor.webp)

![The same starter in a steel pot.](/images/editorial/cotegipe/fermento-panela.webp)

The corn sat there until it turned into something else.
By the end it was so sweet that wasps and bees hovered all around it.

I went looking for the name afterwards, in papers and theses.

The literature describes the same gesture under another name, fermento caipira: cane juice and ground corn, fed every day for five to seven days, with the yeast already living in the juice, the equipment and the vats.

A paper from UFSCar, the federal university of São Carlos, credits the flavour of the drink to those wild yeasts, and even to bacteria.
The same paper notes the cost: with that many contaminants, quality is hard to control.

**Pé de cocho I found written nowhere.**

`FRASE GIGANTE, TELA INTEIRA`

> # The recipe lives with whoever makes it.

## THE OAK

After the still, the tanks and the barrels.
This is where Ouro Especial comes from, aged in oak, 45%.

In a 2010 thesis at the same university, cachaças made with fermento caipira came out of the oak smelling different from those made with commercial yeast.
All of them within the law.
