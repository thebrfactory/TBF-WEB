> A .TBF. tem um ano. Seu acervo tem sete. Antes da empresa existir, já havia anos de pesquisa, referências e repertório esperando para serem descobertos.

Treze mil arquivos: fotografia, vídeo, print de tela, reunião gravada.
Numa noite de agosto colocamos uma máquina para ouvir todos eles.
Ninguém nunca tinha ouvido esse acervo inteiro: quarenta e três horas de áudio, trinta e oito delas de fala.

A ferramenta foi o Whisper, modelo aberto de transcrição, rodando dentro do nosso Mac. Sem nuvem.
Começou às onze da noite e terminou às seis da manhã.
Leu os 3.132 arquivos que tinham áudio dentro, e não falhou em nenhum.

A gente queria poder buscar dentro do que foi dito.
**O que voltou foi a voz de outra pessoa.**

`NUMERO GRANDE`

Reel salvo, curso de terceiro, podcast, tutorial.
A nossa própria câmera responde por duas horas e meia. A última hora e meia nunca foi classificada.
O que ela filma é festa, ensaio, viagem. Ela filma gesto.

`FRASE GIGANTE, TELA INTEIRA`

> # A gente montou um acervo da própria voz e achou outra pessoa falando dentro dele.

Direção de arte no TikTok, tutorial de After Effects, reel de vendas, uma reza em hebraico.
Os poucos clipes em inglês que saíram da nossa câmera são música tocando numa festa.
**Nada disso foi dito para alguém. É o registro do que esta label assiste.**

## CRITÉRIO

Dar o acervo de comer à máquina foi metade do trabalho.
A outra metade foi ensinar a ela o que esta label recusa:
manifesto é memória, e memória falha a alguma taxa, sempre em silêncio.

Então aqui as regras deixaram de ser lembradas e passaram a ser rodadas.
GUARD é um script pequeno que fica entre o trabalho e o mundo.
Um recusa o traço que esta label não usa. Outro bloqueia o caminho para produção.
**Outro conta quantas das nossas regras são código, e quantas ainda são só esperança.**

`FRASE GIGANTE, TELA INTEIRA`

> # Cultura que não vira código é decoração.

**Com um contrapeso, aprendido na marra: guard que chuta é pior que guard nenhum.**
Um desses guards tocou num arquivo que só tinha sido lido, não escrito.
Alarme que toca no arquivo errado ensina todo mundo a ignorar alarme.
Então regra nova só entra se nascer com teste, ou se aposentar outra.

A regra mais antiga daqui é a mais barata: sinal que já existe vence sinal novo.
A câmera já escreve a data, a lente, as coordenadas.
O campo que dizia de quem era cada voz estava parado no catálogo o tempo todo.

## O ERRO

**O alarme que toca no arquivo errado tem um gêmeo: o que nunca toca.**
Na mesma noite em que o acervo aprendeu a responder, ele também mentiu.
Nome de arquivo com acento pode ser guardado de dois jeitos diferentes.
Nosso banco usava um. O script que o alimentava usava o outro.
Na tela eles são idênticos. Byte a byte, não.

`NUMERO GRANDE`

> ## 44
> arquivos que ganharam o texto e continuaram invisíveis para a busca

A gente pegou antes de aplicar o lote, e consertou.
Se não tivesse pego, a busca por esses arquivos devolveria nada, e resultado vazio é idêntico a acervo vazio.
**Teríamos concluído que o acervo era vazio justo no lugar onde ele era mais cheio.**

# THE LABEL THAT DISCOVERED ITSELF.

**Seven years of archives revealed a version of the creative company nobody had seen.**

> AI handed everyone the same tools. So the advantage went back to being repertoire. Then the archive answered how much of ours was actually ours.

`BLOCO GRUDADO NA TELA`

_SÃO PAULO, AUGUST 2026_

> .TBF. is one year old. Its archive is seven. Before the company existed there were already years of research and references.

Thirteen thousand files: photographs, videos, screen grabs, recorded meetings.
One night in August we set a machine to listen to all of it.
Nobody had ever heard the whole archive: forty-three hours of audio, thirty-eight of them speech.

The tool was Whisper, an open transcription model, running on our own Mac. No cloud.
It started at eleven at night and finished at six in the morning.
It read the 3,132 files that had audio, and failed on none.

We wanted to search inside what was said.
**What came back was somebody else's voice.**

`NUMERO GRANDE`

> ## 370,700
> words said out loud across seven years

`NUMERO GRANDE`

> ## 14,135
> of them said into our own camera

**Thirty-four of the thirty-eight spoken hours came from people who do not work here.**
Saved reels, other people's courses, podcasts, tutorials.
Our own camera accounts for two and a half. The last hour and a half was never classified.
What it films is a party, a shoot, a trip. It films gesture.

`FRASE GIGANTE, TELA INTEIRA`

> # We built an archive of our own voice and found somebody else talking in it.

Inside those hours, one in every seven words was said in English.
Nobody here knew that number existed.

And that English is almost all borrowed: of 55,260 words spoken in English, 50,582 come from saved material.
Art direction on TikTok, an After Effects tutorial, a sales reel, a prayer in Hebrew.
In the few English clips our own camera shot, the English is music at a party.
**None of it was addressed to anyone. It is a record of what this label watches.**

## ARCHIVE

`IMAGEM`

![Field of 3,132 marks, one per audio file, coloured by what was said inside it.](/images/editorial/substrato-capa.webp)

The finding, drawn. Every mark is one file: parchment for Portuguese, amber for English, grey for the other sixteen languages, near-black for the 879 where nobody speaks. The grid is 87 by 36, which is exactly 3,132. Made by a script that reads the database.

_.TBF. inc_

The files had always been there, thirteen thousand of them, across seven years and four business units.
That night we gained no files. We gained a way to ask.

Before, you opened folders and tried to remember filenames.
Now you type "show me the starred photos from the client" and the archive answers.
The search looks inside what was said and inside what is written on screen.

`PARES DE NEGACAO`

- An archive is not a repertoire. / An archive is what you kept. A repertoire is what you can connect.

## THE SAMENESS

Everyone says AI made the tools the same. It made the inputs the same too.

`ANTES E DEPOIS`

- The same public corpus.
- The same references, three clicks deep.
- The same trend report, read by everyone.
- The same question, phrased the same way.

- Seven years of recordings nobody else has.
- What we refused, and the day we refused it.
- The first client, who is still a client.
- Our mistakes, dated and kept.

Everyone feeds the machine what everyone has, and the machine hands back the average.
**What only we have fits in 14,135 words. Our own tape counted them.**
We spend our days in the same corpus as everybody else. The difference is that ours got written down.
Nobody escapes the average by writing a prettier prompt.

## CRITERION

Feeding the machine our own archive was half the job.
The other half was teaching it what this label refuses:
a manifesto is memory, and memory fails at some rate, and never out loud.

So here the rules stopped being remembered and started being run.
A GUARD is a small script that sits between the work and the world.
One refuses the dash this label does not use. One blocks the road to production.
**One counts how many of our rules are code, and how many are still hope.**

`FRASE GIGANTE, TELA INTEIRA`

> # Culture that does not become code is decoration.

**One counterweight, learned the hard way: a guard that guesses is worse than no guard at all.**
One of those guards once rang on a file that had only been read, not written.
An alarm that rings on the wrong file teaches everyone to ignore alarms.
So a new rule only enters if it is born with a test, or retires another one.

The oldest rule here is the cheapest: a signal that already exists beats a new signal.
Cameras already write the date, the lens, the coordinates.
The field that told us whose voice each file held had been sitting in the catalogue the whole time.

## THE ERROR

**The alarm that rings on the wrong file has a twin: the one that never rings at all.**
The same night the archive learned to answer, it also lied.
A file name with an accent can be stored two different ways.
Our database used one. The script feeding it used the other.
On screen they are identical. Byte by byte they are not.

`NUMERO GRANDE`

> ## 44
> files that got their text and stayed invisible to the search

We caught it before the batch was applied, and fixed it.
Had we not, a search for those files would have returned nothing, and an empty result looks exactly like an empty archive.
**We would have concluded the archive was empty in the place where it was fullest.**

## AUTHORSHIP

The machine did seven hours of work that night.
**What thirty-four hours of borrowed speech mean for this label is still open.**
Producing got cheap. Choosing got expensive.

Because the machine does not choose.
It has no provenance, and provenance cannot be generated.
**It widens the field. Somebody still has to frame the shot.**

Inside those 370,700 words, recorded on 27 March 2025:

`FRASE GIGANTE, TELA INTEIRA`

> # "People keep asking how I do my work. I just answer: by paying attention."

Ninety-eight seconds of tape, said in Portuguese, filed under a portrait shoot, unheard for seventeen months.
**It took a machine reading forty-three hours to hand back one sentence we already owned.**

`FECHO`

> The archive answered: 14,135 of the 370,700 words are ours. What to do with the rest is not its question.
